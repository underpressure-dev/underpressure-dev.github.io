FPV and drones

Ten years of FPV, now as expertise for you.

We no longer film ourselves. We pass on what we have learned with racing drones since 2016: in consulting, in development and in talks.

01

From hobby to record flight

Racing drones fly where other cameras cannot go: close, fast and precise.

For years we flew with our own components and our own software, from the early days of racing-drone footage to record flights over the Alps, the Matterhorn and the Eiger north face. Large companies and car brands used this footage; the references are listed below.

Today we no longer film ourselves. We pass this knowledge on: anyone who uses, develops or procures drones benefits from ten years of practice at the edge of what is possible.

Drones of our own design are on permanent display in Swiss museums today. We also give talks there.

04

PC-7 Ultra Long Range

1.54 metres of wingspan, 600 kilometres of range. A Pilatus PC-7 model, built without compromise.

600 kilometres in one flight. That is Lucerne to Bern, on to Geneva, through the Valais and back over the Alps to Lucerne. No landing, no refuelling.

On routes like this there is no second chance. So nothing is left to chance: almost all connections soldered rather than plugged, separate power for engine and electronics, electronic switches and a kill switch that stops the engine at any time.

The autopilot flies the route itself, waypoint by waypoint. Even if the radio link drops, it stays in control. Flown by hand in acro mode, it reaches up to 200 km/h.

Petrol instead of batteries, because only that makes the distance possible: a two-stroke with 15 cc and 2.4 hp. The fuel sits in a fuel bladder and it runs on alkylate petrol. The aircraft smells of nothing.

The second version is already planned.
