FATHOM The autopilot for submarines.

Firmware, control, mission planning and configurator for remote-controlled and autonomous submarines, developed by us from scratch. FATHOM stabilises, holds depth, runs missions on its own where no radio can reach, and finds its way back with GPS at the surface. Missions are planned on the national map with lake relief, settings are made over USB or the boat's own WLAN.

01

The principle

Built like iNav. Made for under water.

FATHOM is our own firmware, written from scratch, on an RP2040. The BNO055 measures attitude and heading, the MS5837 measures pressure and therefore depth. From these FATHOM controls pitch, heading and depth, 100 times per second.

Firmware, control, protocol, configurator, mission planner and map integration all come from one hand. The planner works with the same limits as the boat, and the parameter list comes straight from the firmware source code.

Anyone who flies planes or multirotors with iNav knows the principle: firmware in the vehicle, a configurator on the PC, clear modes, a mission planner and a failsafe that takes over in an emergency. Under water more comes into play: buoyancy and ballast, pressure instead of altitude, and no radio below the surface. The boat has to be able to decide on its own.

We know iNav from our own practice: our long-range PC-7 flies with it.

02

Six modes, one clear sequence

From driving by hand to the autonomous mission. And above it all the failsafe, which always takes priority.

01

Manual

Direct control from the transmitter. Rudders and thrust follow the pilot.

02

Stabilised

A PID controller holds the pitch angle, with the bow planes mixed in.

03

Depth

FATHOM holds the set depth with dive planes, sink-rate damping and ballast. When the boat stops, the pump holds the depth.

04

Mission

The boat runs a planned mission step by step, autonomously and, if you choose, entirely without radio.

05

Return to home (RTH)

With GPS, the boat returns to its starting point at the surface. Under water it never navigates by GPS: it surfaces first, waits for a valid fix and then heads home. Optionally also automatically after the mission.

06

Failsafe

On a leak, critical battery, depth limit, sensor failure or loss of radio, FATHOM takes over: empty the ballast, nose up 10°, 15 % throttle, surface. On radio loss, optionally with return to home.

03

New in version 0.5

Mission planner with lake chart

Plan the dive at home on screen.

You set the start and waypoints on the swisstopo national map, with the lake relief from measured lake depths underneath. One click turns this into the complete mission: turn onto the course, dive, run straight, with the time from distance and speed.

At the lake: load the mission into the boat over WLAN, arm and start it over Crossfire. The boat does the rest on its own.

  • National map or aerial image from swisstopo, alternatively OpenStreetMap
  • Lake relief from swissBATHY3D, the federal lake depth data, with adjustable opacity
  • Depth and speed for every waypoint, markers can be dragged
  • Optionally beacon at the end and return to home after the mission
  • Save the plan as a file, it stays stored in the browser

There is no GPS under water. The boat runs by heading and time and does not compensate for current and drift. The speed factor is determined with a measured distance.

Know what happens before the start

  • 3D preview of the track, with playback
  • Depth profile and top view
  • Duration, distance, maximum depth and energy demand before the start
  • Warnings before anything can go wrong: depth above the limit, speed above the limit, too many steps
  • Send, store in the boat, read back, over USB or WLAN

04

Navigation and black box

Back to the starting point

GPS only works at the surface. So FATHOM only uses it there: the boat surfaces, waits for a valid fix and then returns to the starting point.

  • Home is set on arming, otherwise at the first GPS fix at the surface, or by hand
  • Return to home in phases: surface, wait for GPS, travel, arrived
  • If you choose, the failsafe starts the return to home on radio loss
  • Position plot without internet: north up, grid in metres, distance and bearing to home

Black box: know afterwards what happened

Under water you cannot see the boat. So the black box records every dive. Afterwards you read everything out over WLAN and see in the browser, step by step, what the boat measured and controlled.

  • Records while armed, 1 to 20 times per second: depth, attitude, servos, battery, GPS and leak probe
  • Read out only when disarmed, over USB or WLAN, with a checksum
  • Charts for depth, attitude, heading, thrust, ballast and rudders, with zoom
  • GPS track, duration, maximum depth, lowest battery voltage and failsafe events
  • Export as CSV, save the raw data as a file
05

Safety that thinks ahead

Failsafe takes priority

Leaks, battery, depth limit, sensor failure and radio loss are monitored continuously. One trigger is enough, and the boat surfaces.

Leaks in two stages

Two probes in two places, the wetter one counts. Small leak: emergency ascent, at the surface the boat switches off. Major leak: emergency stop, everything powerless, the beacon raises the alarm. Arming again only after a restart.

Status LEDs on the hull

Green: a short flash means ready, steady light armed, fast blinking mission. Red flashes count the fault: 1 battery, 2 leak, 3 depth, 4 sensor, 5 radio loss. Steady red means emergency stop.

WLAN only when disarmed

When the boat is armed, the firmware switches the WLAN off and keeps it off, even against accidental switching on. Arming and mission start never work over WLAN.

Safe test mode

Servos, motor and pump can only be driven directly when disarmed. If the signal stops for 1.5 s, the firmware stops motor and pump.

Limits in software

Rudder deflection, pitch, throttle and depth are limited, identically in the firmware and in the mission planner.

06

New in version 0.5

The beacon: recovery even when everything fails

The submarine is sinking? You see it flashing.

The beacon is a separate pod with its own battery, its own computer and a powerful 3-watt strobe, plus an RGB pixel for red warning signals. It is connected to the boat by a single data line.

The boat reports five times per second. If the report stays away for 8 seconds although the boat was last armed, the pod raises the alarm on its own. This also covers the case in which the electronics in the boat have failed completely.

A parameter sets when the beacon flashes: only at the end of a mission and on command, on faults, additionally at the surface after the mission, or always while armed.

07

WLAN on board

Configure it on the shore, with a phone. No cable, no app, no internet.

A small radio module sits in the boat, a XIAO ESP32 C3. It opens its own WLAN and serves the configurator itself. Phone, tablet or laptop connect straight to the boat, and the pressure hull stays closed.

The map needs internet, the boat WLAN has none. So you plan at home, then switch to the boat WLAN and upload the mission.

How to connect

  1. Switch the boat on. It must be disarmed.
  2. Join the WLAN FATHOM-XXXX.
  3. Open http://192.168.4.1 in the browser.
  4. Press «Per WLAN verbinden» (connect via WLAN).

This does not work from underpressure.ch: the site is encrypted, and the browser does not allow a connection from there into the boat’s unencrypted network. That is why the boat carries the configurator itself.

Over WLAN

  • Read and write parameters
  • Send missions and store them in the boat
  • Read out the black box
  • Tests and calibration
  • Live telemetry at 10 Hz

Only over Crossfire or USB

  • Arming
  • Mission start
  • Bootloader and firmware update

The configurator

One tool for everything, right in the browser and with no installation.

The configurator reads the parameter list directly from the firmware source code when it is built. So boat and configurator always speak the same language, over USB just as over WLAN.

  • Live graphs for attitude, heading, depth, battery, leak and ballast
  • Calibration: depth, attitude, IMU profile, battery voltage, servo centre, end points and direction

  • Navigation: set home, start and end return to home
  • Black box: load a recording, charts, GPS track and CSV export

  • Terminal for every protocol command

09

Design values

Example: a submarine as a dive buddy

Our test vehicle: a submarine that dives with you.

This is the boat on which we develop and trial FATHOM: a remote-controlled Alfa-class submarine at 1:120 scale, 67 cm long, built for Lake Lucerne and designed for 60 metres. Meant as a dive buddy that comes along under water.

Closed once, never opened again

The hull is printed from resin by SLA, reinforced with glass fibre and bonded by hand. Inside sits the pressure hull: a tube wrapped in GRP and closed with custom-made end caps.

Once the boat is closed, it stays closed. No USB socket, no connectors, nothing that could leak at 60 metres. Firmware and radio module are loaded over USB before the boat is sealed. After that everything runs over WLAN: parameters, missions and black box. Nobody has to open the boat for a new mission.

Propulsion and control

  • Wet-running brushless motor T-Motor AS2304, the propeller turns freely in the water flow
  • Ballast pump on the peristaltic principle for fine trimming
  • Three servos for bow planes, stern planes and rudder, oil-filled to compensate pressure down to 60 metres
  • Remote control over TBS Crossfire, the long-range radio from model aviation
  • Status LEDs outside on the hull, visible under water

Under construction

Status

FATHOM is under construction. Firmware and planner are tested in simulation, the tests with the hardware in the lake will follow. All figures for the boat are design values.

It is tested on a PC, with test models instead of hardware: the firmware core with protocol, arming, failsafe, test mode and a complete mission against a boat model, plus GPS parsing, return to home, the black box format, the leak logic, the signal to the beacon and the status LED patterns, the locks for the radio port, and the configurator over a simulated WLAN bridge.

Not yet tested: the firmware on real hardware, the programs of the radio module (written, not yet compiled) and of the beacon pod, the WLAN in the real boat, watertightness at 60 metres and all tests in the water. The controller values are starting values and will be tuned in the water.
