Aerospace · Research and development

Our satellites fly with the wind.

High-altitude balloons carry our electronics up to 38 km and across whole continents in days, for a fraction of the cost of a satellite. We develop trackers, radio, cameras and ground stations ourselves.

A high-altitude balloon is a satellite that costs little and gets quickly to where it is needed.

It launches in hours instead of years, rides the high-altitude winds across countries and oceans and collects data where no other technology reaches. Everything it measures and sees reaches the ground by radio only.

02

Built for up there

This is what we are proud of: electronics that manage entirely on their own up there. Every flight is a stress test we cannot repeat.

Cold

The electronics start up and transmit at temperatures far below zero.

Thin air

Hardly any cooling, big differences between sun and shade. Every component has to cope.

Unfiltered sun

Above the clouds, radiation hits housings, cables and boards at full strength.

Power from above

The WSPR trackers live on solar power, day after day, for weeks.

Every gram counts

Whatever is not essential stays on the ground. Light and still sturdy, that is the real craft.

No second attempt

After launch nobody can step in. Every solder joint, every antenna and every line of firmware has to be right beforehand.

03

Two systems of our own

Shortwave via the ionosphere

WSPR tracker

A Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) with GPS and a temperature sensor, powered by solar cells. It transmits a few milliwatts on shortwave, in the 20 and 10 metre bands.

The signals are reflected back to earth by the ionosphere, several hundred kilometres up, and skip across thousands of kilometres. Receiving stations around the world report what they hear: the balloon's position, altitude and temperature.

The trackers ride the high-altitude winds at 10 to 12 km, for weeks and across continents.

  • RP2040
  • pico-WSPRer
  • GPS ATGM336H
  • DS18B20
  • 20 m and 10 m
  • Solar

Atmospheric Upper Altitude Recon Asset

AURA

Our camera system for high altitude. A camera takes pictures, a LoRa radio module sends them to the ground station on 433 MHz, and GPS provides the position.

We develop the software for payload, ground station and analysis on the PC ourselves. The highest flight so far reached 38 km, well into the stratosphere.

  • OV5640
  • LoRa RFM96W
  • 433 MHz
  • GPS
  • CircuitPython

What this technology can be used for

What we develop for our balloons can be used for many tasks on the ground and in the air.

01

Data from the atmosphere

Capture temperature, pressure, humidity and other readings where no fixed station exists. For research, weather and climate projects.

02

Receiving wildlife transmitters

A balloon at high altitude hears far. It can pick up the signals of transmitters on wild animals over large areas and pass them on, even where there is no receiving station on the ground.

03

Sensors without mobile coverage

LoRa transmits readings over kilometres with very little power. For places without mobile coverage: water levels, huts, construction sites and installations in the Alps.

04

Developing and testing radio

Test antennas, range and transmission methods under real conditions, from LoRa to shortwave.

05

Images from the stratosphere

Footage from up to 38 km altitude for research, education, media and advertising.

06

Components under extreme conditions

Test electronics and materials in cold and low pressure, under conditions close to space.

06

Security and defence

The same technology is of interest to emergency services, authorities and armed forces: links where there are none, and an eye on signals that cannot be trusted.

Radio relay at altitude

From high altitude the line of sight reaches hundreds of kilometres. As a flying repeater, a balloon can extend radio links across mountains and valleys, where no connection is possible on the ground.

Detecting GPS interference

GPS receivers at altitude as a sensor network: detecting and reporting interference and forged satellite signals, so-called spoofing, across large areas.

Ready in hours

No launch pad and no satellite contract. A balloon is in the air within hours, for situations where speed matters.

Details are discussed in person only.
